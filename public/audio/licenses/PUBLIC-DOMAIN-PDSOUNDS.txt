Public-domain author dedications: PDSounds field recordings
Evidence retrieved: 2026-09-14

Source:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-pdsounds.org

The source states:

"This audio file come from pdsounds.org and has been released into the
public domain by its author."

It quotes the PDSounds Help page:

"Everyone who logs in may upload his recordings of the world. If you record
for pdsounds, you must agree to release the audio files you make into the
public domain.
This means that everyone can download the sounds and use them at will - use
them for education, use them to create art, use them for commercial purposes
too - there are no copyrights, no strings attached to the files."

Original Help URL recorded by Wikimedia Commons:
http://www.pdsounds.org/help/what_can_i_find_here

Each included file also carries its own named-author dedication:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Template:PD-author

For example, the fountain file page states:

"This work has been released into the public domain by its author, aldor.
This applies worldwide.
In some countries this may not be legally possible; if so:
aldor grants anyone the right to use this work for any purpose, without any
conditions, unless such conditions are required by law."

Example source:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fountain_in_toulouse.ogg

The original sound creators, not Wikimedia uploader Fæ, are credited.
Each asset's adjacent evidence text and sources/<id>.json preserve its
own author, original download link, permission statement, description,
named-author license declaration, and fetched source-file SHA-256.

These recordings are labelled "Public domain (author dedication)", not CC0.
No inference is made from a composition's age. They are field and object
recordings explicitly released by their recording authors.

The current availability of the retired PDSounds site is not guaranteed.
The retained evidence is Wikimedia Commons' per-file documentation of
the original dedications, not a claim that the retired site was reachable.
