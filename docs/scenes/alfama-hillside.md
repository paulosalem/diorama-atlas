# Alfama

**Scene:** `alfama-hillside` · **Region:** Lisboa, Portugal · **Status:** bundled collection

Public visits: [Landscape](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=alfama-hillside&inspect=1)
and [Miniature](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=alfama-hillside&version=miniature).
Both representations share the actual neighborhood geometry. This is not a
survey of a real street or a substitute for residents' accounts.

## Cultural proposition

An imagined contemporary residential hillside informed by Alfama: small
limewashed houses, stair streets, irregular landings, narrow passages and
domestic thresholds. The ordinary activity is washing hung to dry, tending
flower pots and preparing a modest afternoon meal. Warm light reaches the
upper walls while cool shade fills the lanes.

The user's "beautiful poverty" is interpreted through modest scale, repairs,
reuse and attentive domestic care, not as a claim that deprivation is desirable
or that all Alfama households live alike. No ruined-neighborhood spectacle,
permanent festival, invented folklore or branded tourist restaurant. Beauty
belongs to spatial ingenuity, material texture and people's care for a place.

## Spatial sequence and representations

A lower cobbled landing leads into a stair lane between narrow houses. A first
flight reaches an offset landing; a second turns toward the upper alley.
Side routes disclose an inhabited doorway and a small planting/washing court
through a building passage. A higher return view reveals the roofscape and
the route just climbed. Not every turn is visible from the arrival.

The miniature physically sections the same neighborhood into two closed
landscape pieces. Orbit and zoom reveal roofs, backs, thick walls, stair
foundations and domestic corners. This interpretive separation is a display
device, not a crack or geological claim about Lisbon.

Implemented views: `arrival`, `stairs`, `passage`, `courtyard`, `tiles`, `roofs`,
`upperCourt`, `backLane`, `street` and `daily`.
The miniature adds `whole`, for ten landscape and eleven miniature views.
The upper-court and back-lane views reuse the recorded court and stair passages
in both languages. The two street/bicycle inspection views are explicitly
non-narrated in both representations; no new paid recording was necessary.
Both retain direct links and the shared
Landscape/Miniature switch.

The spatial revision replaces parallel stair avenues with seven flights:
north then west at the lower elbow, a diagonal ascent followed by a westward
return, a second west/north back lane, and a long return behind the eastern
washing court. Intermediate landings at 1.2 and 3.6 metres interrupt the
0/2.4/4.8-metre bands. Terraces have shaped edges and stair notches rather
than burying the turning flights inside rectangular hillside blocks.
Fifteen houses face their own lane or court, including east/west-facing
frontages, a north-facing return and oblique parcels. A second upper court
is enclosed by three differently oriented houses. This is a connected,
authored neighborhood, not randomly rotated buildings or a surveyed Alfama plan.
The six original cameras remain unchanged for honest comparisons.
The miniature's crosswise separation now falls between the two building groups,
at Z=3.1, rather than cutting a rotated house. Each side retains physically
capped terrain. A continuous full-scale route is deliberately separated at
that display cut, not represented as a walkable bridge across empty air.

Eight of the fifteen households now belong to four attached masonry groups:
two pairs share a single full-depth party wall, while two fitted infill wings
join differently oriented neighbors. The lower wing follows the unequal parcel
edges; the court junction closes the accidental daylight slit beside the ochre
door without closing the adjacent arched passage. Existing walls own their
shared boundaries, with no duplicate wall built by the infill. Windows below
the joining roof are omitted on covered sides. The infills have floors,
foundations, exposed masonry returns and fitted tiled roofs, not thin screens
placed in front of gaps. This is architectural modeling, not a structural
engineering or seismic-safety claim.

Stair margins are fitted to their adjoining construction, not just connected
along the walking centerline. The diagonal flight has a shaped masonry junction
to its landing, the house back and the upper terrace. Stepped backings close the
narrow trenches beside the westward flights and rear retaining wall; landing
returns close the remaining inaccessible notches. These are closed, grounded
solids with clipped, beveled calçada, not screens or props hiding empty corners.
Original tread widths, circulation, house apertures and cameras are retained.
The infill roofs have 100 mm closed support decks with shaped surfaces and
locally aligned tile courses following their unequal parcel edges.

## View sequence

The six distinct guided passages follow this sequence:

- `arrival`: the lower lane and an introduction to the imagined neighborhood.
- `stairs`: turns, landings and the experience of moving uphill.
- `passage`: thick masonry and the inhabited court beyond.
- `courtyard`: washing, plants and domestic care.
- `tiles`: limestone thresholds, tile and repaired joinery.
- `roofs`: the close-built neighborhood seen from above.

The upper-court and back-lane cameras reuse the court and stair passages.
The lower-street and bicycle cameras are additional silent inspection views.
The miniature inherits these cameras and adds its own narrated whole-piece view.

## Construction, matter and evidence

- **Arrival:** irregular building heights, warm white/ochre/blush plaster,
  limestone reveals, restrained blue-and-white tile, dark green joinery,
  overlapping curved terracotta roof tiles and supported iron balconies.
  Avoid a symmetric townhouse parade or a monumental staircase.
- **Connected space:** genuinely open passage and furnished domestic room,
  offset sightlines and walk-scale landings. Floors, walls, steps and thresholds
  must meet; ornament and washing must not block circulation.
- **Closest detail:** rounded and selectively chipped stone nosings, small
  calçada joints, individual glaze variation, door panels, hinges, handles,
  downpipe joints, lantern brackets, plant stems, hollow pots, cloth hems and
  pegs. Natural variation is not blanket dirt.

Roof planes close the buildings; layered tiles and ridge caps explain water
shedding. Floors separate storeys. Windows have actual apertures, jamb depth,
recessed frames and interior curtains rather than dark stickers. One ground-floor
room contains a table, chairs, dishes and an illustrated pantry label.
Flowering pots gather at usable edges rather than filling the stairs.
Supported balcony pots, herb shelves, brooms and open slatted crates accompany
an outdoor meal, a potting bench with a hollow watering can, a woven laundry
basket and a towel resting over a chair. Smaller plants have herb-like foliage
rather than repeating the same flowering plant everywhere. Folded and rumpled
linen, crumbs and a few fallen leaves suggest specific tasks, not blanket clutter.

Two parked bicycles, one carrying groceries in an open woven basket, sit beside
the middle lane and lower houses. A compact hatchback occupies the level eastern
lower street with a plausible access route; no car sits on a stair-only terrace.
Seven new containers include handled glazed jars, a terracotta herb trough,
low succulent bowls and galvanized buckets. Thick walls and actual interiors,
fine ceramic throwing relief and restrained finish variation distinguish their
construction. A cloth shopping trolley, empty bucket and coiled hose extend
shopping and gardening activity without filling the routes.
The car's program-drawn `DA-26-04` plate is fictional original lettering, not a
copied registration or a claim that the generic vehicle is a branded model.

The flowering pots now have curved, scalloped and zoned leaves connected by
petioles to branching stems, with small cupped petals gathered into flower
clusters. Botanical photographs guide those shapes without asserting an exact
Alfama cultivar. The two tiled-doorstep pots stand clear of the wall.
Calçada uses eight physically different stone profiles, unequal corner cuts,
shallow surface relief and irregular bedding courses. Filled joints sit just
below the stone tops rather than leaving the stones perched above deep gaps.
Roof tiles taper along their length so overlapping courses have real relief
instead of merging into continuous tubes. Concave channels close the spaces
between cover tiles, and courses end at their supporting roof planes.
Compatible paving is instanced
across the neighborhood; the extra detail does not require one draw per stone.

Limewash has shallow modeled relief, spatially varied pigment loss, licensed
fine normal detail and restrained branching fractures. Better-maintained walls
remain distinct from more weathered ones. Masonry openings reserve the stone
reveals so their surfaces do not flicker against coincident plaster.

The latest material pass adds coherent broad pigment variation, chalkier
limewash, localized ground splash, runoff beneath actual sill ends and
occasional repair patches. Roof pieces vary in firing tone and use physically
scaled scanned grain; their finish is separate from pottery so plants and pots
retain their original arrangement and character. Azulejos vary per piece while
preserving their ornament, and exposed chimney plaster uses the shared weathering
finish. These are restrained differences in exposure and maintenance, not a
uniform layer of dirt or newly modeled ceramic chips.

Both landscape and miniature now use a warmer direct sun (`#ffc58a`, intensity
3.8). Cool landscape fill remains at 0.60, keeping shaded courts and passages
readable. Exposure, original cameras and photographic-sky solar alignment are
unchanged: this is warmer authored daylight, not a sunset simulation or a
full-frame orange filter. Other miniatures retain their existing lighting.

The scene has no water surface: no canal, fountain or ocean recording is needed.
Washing moves slightly around fixed pegs. The licensed shared palette of distant
conversation, dishes and birds is illustrative, not an Alfama field recording.
Sound remains opt-in and follows global mute, page hiding and disposal.

English and Brazilian Portuguese cover the scene, views, details and miniature
story. Fourteen prerecorded passages cover both languages, guided views and details
through exact-text reuse; the two inspection views remain deliberately silent.
They were generated with explicit task-specific consent,
a US$2 ceiling and a durable ledger; recorded usage totaled US$0.0981834.
The first build and three subsequent correction rounds retain matched captures
for both representations. Subsequent layout, construction, material and daily-life
passes retain fresh baselines and their own correction evidence. The scene is
included in the public collection following the user's publication decision and
release verification. Its authored architecture, secondary rooms and vehicles
remain stylized; publication is not a claim of photographic realism or an
exemption from further refinement and human visual judgement.
