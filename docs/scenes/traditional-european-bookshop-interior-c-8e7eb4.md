## Cultural intention

Explore books as handled objects and a shop as a place of browsing, reading,
exchange and care. The late-nineteenth-century premise is a period-oriented
interpretation, not an exact city, bookseller or verified historical inventory.

## Spatial and sensory direction

Timber shelves, a reading desk, a window corner and a furnished secondary
reading room create successive depths. Books, correspondence, spectacles and
binding materials should support distinct activities rather than merely fill
empty shelves.

Visible covers, spines and open pages need actual image content and physical
binding/paper. Vary leather, paper edges, wood and metal response. Window light
and warm lamps should make reading inviting while shelf recesses remain quieter.
Sparse page-handling sound must not become constant rustling.

## View sequence

`room` → `desk` → `shelves` → `window` → `passage`.

## Preserve and extend

Keep the long saved ID and title for existing links. Add bibliographic or
historical claims only with support; original fictional print layouts are not
facsimiles of authenticated period books. Preserve a calm route through the
shelves and a genuinely open connection to the reading room.
