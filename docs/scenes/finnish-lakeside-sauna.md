# Finnish Lakeside Sauna

**Scene:** `finnish-lakeside-sauna` · **Region:** Finnish Lakeland, near Saimaa

[Enter the sauna](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=finnish-lakeside-sauna&inspect=1&version=interior)
· [Turn the miniature](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=finnish-lakeside-sauna&version=miniature)

## Cultural intention

An imagined contemporary household sauna at a summer cottage, during a quiet
late-summer afternoon. The subject is a familiar cycle of heating, washing,
resting and cooling, not competitive heat endurance or an exclusive wellness
retreat. Sauna culture includes many building types and ways of bathing; this
small wood-fired example is not presented as the most authentic type.

The visitor should understand the relationship between the kiuas, its heated
stones, water and löyly; the different bench heights; and the sequence from
warm enclosure to changing room, porch and lake. A birch whisk is called
vasta in much of eastern Finland and vihta elsewhere. It is an optional,
variable practice, not compulsory equipment or a medical treatment.

The building, layout and household are fictional. The map identifies a region
of inspiration, not a surveyed address. No community endorsement is implied.

## Spatial and sensory direction

A compact timber building has a roughly 3.6 by 3.8 metre bathing room and an
adjoining 2.2 metre-wide changing room. A real, outward-opening internal door
connects them. The second doorway leads to a sheltered porch, steps, a worn
path and a supported timber jetty. Doors, reveals, windows, thresholds and
ceiling penetrations must be modeled openings, not dark or transparent patches
over a solid wall. Windows are modest domestic apertures, not a panoramic
glass facade borrowed from a commercial spa.

The sauna has pale, maintained bench timber, warmer wall boards, a tiled wet
floor and localized dampness. Heat shields and a protective rail distinguish
the stove area from circulation. The heater has a real firebox, stone chamber,
grate, flue and fittings. Its dimensions and protective details are an artistic
interpretation, not installation or fire-safety guidance.

Tiered benches need slatted seats, rounded edges, supported legs and rails,
real gaps, visible joinery and safe-looking access. Wood varies within and
between pieces without blanket soot or exaggerated decay. A rumpled bench
cloth, wooden headrest, water bucket, hollow ladle and tied birch whisk suggest
a pause in use. The changing room holds towels, shoes, seating and drinking
water; the porch has a sheltered fuel stack and a place to sit.

Birches, connected small foliage, shore grasses, reeds, embedded stones and
bare routes occupy the same continuous terrain. A single physical lake surface
meets the shore and jetty. Its restrained irregular movement and reflections
must remain coherent from the sauna window, porch and water's edge. Trees must
not become disconnected leaf clouds or obscure the route. No unexplained
hovering terrace, water beneath interior floors or duplicated reflection plane.

Low oblique daylight enters real windows and open doors. The bathing room is
quieter and darker than the lake, but its construction remains readable. Use
restrained motivated steam, fire and anchored foliage movement, with a genuine
reduced-motion freeze. Optional recorded fire and very quiet garden ambience
are illustrative; they are not Finnish field recordings or a substitute for
lake-water audio. All sound remains opt-in.

## View sequence

`room` → `heater` → `benches` → `changing` → `passage` → `shore`.

The miniature uses the same building, furniture, trees and immediate shore.
Its actual roof, ceiling and selected exterior walls move upward and sideways,
exposing the bathing room and connected changing room. The jetty, banks and
closed underside remain complete. Preserve both whole-object and focused
views, orbit/zoom and direct switching to the walking-scale environment.
Views whose subjects belong to the moving enclosure must close the shell.

## Evidence and likely failure modes

Inspect the arrival at human eye height, the connected-room and shore sequence,
and close views of stones, slats, vessels, timber corners and cloth contact.
Inspect the miniature closed, opened, reversed and from underneath.

Reject toy-like rounded heater stones, flat boxes used as logs, oversized grain,
uniform orange wood, floating cloth, false bucket bottoms, sealed flues,
unsupported benches, doors colliding with furniture, lake reflections crossing
land and vegetation that blocks the path. Resolve shape and physical contact
before adding more objects or effects. Preserve initial captures and three
complete correction/reinspection rounds, plus comparable performance readings.

The initial research set includes twenty inspected photographs of interiors,
exteriors, reverse/oblique approaches, heater construction, fire, bench joinery,
headrests, whisks, vessels and birch bark. A separately inspected Gallen-Kallela
painting is art, not photographic evidence. Raw sources, licenses, hashes and
observations remain in ignored research outputs. Bundled approved photographs
retain their own accurate attribution. Generic material scans are not scans
of the depicted building.

The timber channels use an aligned CC0 crop of Poly Haven's `raw_plank_wall`,
not a species identification. The distant woodland instances Poly Haven's CC0
`fir_sapling` variants; these generic models are not surveyed Saimaa vegetation.
Their source files, authors, processing and hashes are preserved in
`public/sauna/materials/manifest.json` and `public/sauna/forest/manifest.json`.

The hidden floor plan, foundations, ventilation, exact dimensions and equipment
arrangement are authored interpretation. Do not infer measured construction
from a photograph. Six full-scale views, seven miniature views and four details
share 22 bilingual recordings. Named exterior views close the miniature shell;
the whole-object view supports opening, orbit and zoom. This remains an
interpretive real-time 3D scene, not a photographic or construction survey.
