## Cultural proposition

An anonymous urban house belonging to an imagined wealthy municipal elite
household, not an imperial palace, surveyed Pompeian house, or claim about a
particular historical family. Pompeii, Herculaneum and Oplontis provide comparative
evidence; their surviving objects are not a complete inventory frozen in ordinary
use. The interpretation restores maintained plaster, timber, textiles and food,
not the appearance of an excavated ruin.

The visitor freely walks through connected rooms and examines objects and
explanations. This is not an activity simulation. Household objects do not operate
as appliances, and the inhabitants are suggested through work in progress rather
than invented animated historical people. “Complete” means coverage of domestic
functions and a documented representative inventory, not proof that every object
ever possessed by a Roman aristocrat has been reconstructed.

An early afternoon of meal preparation follows a morning of reception and business.
Light enters the compluvium and peristyle; covered rooms are quieter and darker.
The house is prosperous and maintained, with restrained handling wear. Roman
culture appears through hospitality, patronage, literacy, Greek cultural prestige,
trade, household worship, food, dress, childhood, sanitation and unequal access
to comfort. Women's work and property, freedpeople and enslaved labor must not
disappear behind a generic male householder.

## Materials and visual direction

### The cultivated peristyle

The garden is the household's principal retreat, not four sparsely planted boxes.
Preserve the original garden camera and the circulation around all four beds.
Low clipped evergreen borders frame layered rose and lily planting, with fuller
laurel-like crowns in the rear beds. Deliberate differences in height, leaf shape,
flower color and density make a tended garden rather than repeated procedural
shrubs. These are botanical interpretations, not identified ancient cultivars.

A raised marble labrum, supported on a carved pedestal, receives a modest
bronze bird spout. Its actual basin, waterline, localized disturbance, drain and
support must agree at walking scale. Shaded seating, a reading table, cushions,
watering vessels and cuttings connect leisure with its maintenance. Fluted upper
column shafts, articulated cornices and credited garden painting connect the
planting to the architecture without importing later palace-garden styles.

The House of the Vettii documents an elite garden as a fountain-and-sculpture
focal point. The Archaeological Park's
[House of the Garden of Hercules](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-the-garden-of-hercules/)
reports pollen evidence for roses, violets and lilies in a commercial flower
garden: evidence for available plants, **not** this elite household's exact beds.
The garden fresco from the Villa of Livia supplies credited painted imagery,
not a claim that this Campanian family possessed the original or lived in an
imperial villa. Its museum railings and adjacent walls must not enter the texture.

Lime plaster in warm white, iron-oxide red and ochre; restrained dark dadoes;
black-and-white tessellated floors and opus-signinum-like service surfaces;
terracotta roof tiles, timber shutters and furniture; bronze, iron, glass,
linen and wool. Avoid importing Marrakech ornament, modern printed books,
medieval furnishings or wholesale marble-palace imagery.

Photographs of excavated or museum objects establish construction and silhouettes.
They do not justify copying carbonization, missing roofs or conservation damage
into a living household. Wall paintings may use credited ancient image
reproductions with clear source attribution; they are not evidence that this
invented family owned those paintings.

## Sources and limits

- [Pompeii Archaeological Park: House of the Menander](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-the-menander/):
  elite household, atrium and peristyle, small baths, service area, silverware.
  The page's named ownership is not transferred to this anonymous house.
- [Pompeii Archaeological Park: House of the Vettii](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-the-vettii/):
  wealthy freedpeople, altered plan without a tablinum, garden display and
  household worship. Evidence against a single universal domestic plan.
- [Pompeii Archaeological Park: House of the Ceii](https://pompeiisites.org/en/archaeological-site/house-of-the-ceii/):
  façade, impluvium construction, painted garden, and modern conservation history.
- [Jeffrey Becker, Roman domestic architecture](https://smarthistory.org/roman-domestic-architecture-domus/):
  architectural vocabulary and domestic social power; comparative introduction.
- [Metropolitan Museum: reconstructed couch and footstool](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/249232):
  used cautiously as comparative furniture construction, **not** as a securely
  dated AD 60 furnishing; the museum explicitly notes uncertainty in assembly.
- Licensed photographs and acquisition metadata are retained with the references.
  Inspected evidence includes the Menander peristyle and shrine, Herculaneum's
  carbonized bed, a Pompeian bronze lamp, Boscoreale cooking vessels, carbonized
  bread, and the so-called Sappho portrait with writing equipment.
  The portrait's modern nickname does not identify the sitter as the poet.
- A latrine photograph from the Lupanar informs the seat cavity only; its
  building function is **not** silently reassigned to an aristocratic household.
- A Menander photograph returned by a bath search actually shows a sublevel
  beneath a dining room. It is not accepted as a photograph of a bath.

Private baths, exact furniture placement, living occupants, costume colors,
roof reconstruction, plumbing destinations, room assignments and daily routines
are explicit interpretive choices. Archaeology does not authorize a definitive
“everything a Roman home contained” claim. Modern garden planting is not direct
evidence for ancient plant arrangements.

Papyrus and wax-tablet lettering is original illustrative Latin household
accounting, not a transcription or facsimile. “Ad vilicum” addresses an estate
manager; the commodity lists suggest wine, oil, grain, bread and olives without
claiming recovered quantities or an authentic ancient handwriting sample.
The physical scrolls curl above their supports; hanging tunics have folded front
and back panels, joined sides and shoulders, and open necks, sleeves and hems.
