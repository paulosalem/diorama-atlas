# Samoan Fale and Household Grounds

**Scene:** `samoan-fale` · **Region:** Upolu, Samoa

[Enter the household](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=samoan-fale&inspect=1&version=interior)
· [Turn the miniature](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=samoan-fale&version=miniature)

Both representations share one furnished household assembly. Dimensions and
layout are authored, not measurements of a surveyed building.

## Cultural intention

An imagined contemporary household on Upolu, in the quieter interval between
preparing food and gathering to eat. An open-sided fale, a private living room,
a separate cooking shelter and their shared grounds make the household legible
as a relationship between spaces rather than a single enclosed room.

The subject is everyday care, skilled construction and adaptable shelter, not
a tourist beach bungalow or a staged chiefly ceremony. The visitor should
notice how a generous roof, open sides, woven screens and maintained ground
mediate light, weather and domestic activity. Spaciousness is intentional;
filling the central floor with Western furniture would obscure that lesson.

Fale is not the name of only one building type. Historical sources distinguish
the long afolau, with two lines of substantial interior supports, from the
rounder fale tele with tall central posts. This household pavilion takes its
structural cue from the former. It must not casually combine a long-house
outline with the central-post arrangement of a round council house.

The household, its exact plan and its material combinations are authored.
Contemporary photographs show metal roofs, masonry platforms, painted posts,
gardens and enclosed buildings alongside open fale. A thatched historical
photograph is evidence of construction, not proof that all households today
look that way. No named family, village, rank, sacred seating arrangement or
community endorsement is represented.

## Spatial and sensory direction

The elongated pavilion is roughly 9.6 by six metres, with rounded ends and
a short straight ridge. Its stone-faced platform rises approximately 0.42 m
above a gently draining yard. Broad, fully supported steps connect the front
to the approach; another clear route continues behind the pavilion to the
cooking shelter and private living room. Do not ring the platform with a
decorative barrier that secretly seals those routes.

Two inboard rows of substantial timber supports carry crossbeams and the
ridge-supporting structure. Lower perimeter posts support the eaves. Resolve
the actual bearing surfaces, curved end framing, braces and coconut-fibre
sennit bindings. The selected contemporary interpretation uses a maintained,
muted painted-metal roof: corrugations, shaped end panels, overlaps, ridge
capping, fasteners and physical eave thickness must survive close and oblique
inspection. Historical thatch laths must not be added as unexplained ornament
where a metal-roof assembly does not need them.

Woven coconut-leaf pola screens occupy selected bays, some folded up and one
partly lowered. They need crossing strips, midrib edges, suspension cords,
layered folds and real light gaps, not opaque beige rectangles. They must not
be confused with fine ceremonial mats. Ordinary floor mats remain comparatively
coarse, undecorated and supported; a partially rolled edge reveals their
construction without turning the whole floor into random disorder.

The paused activity is making or repairing a utility coconut-leaf basket.
A partly woven basket, several prepared strips and a completed carrying
basket belong together near the shaded edge, away from circulation. Inspect
real over-and-under strips, the open cavity, the folded base and the rim.
Coconut shells, husks and vessels need their actual volumes and openings.
Do not use a solid ellipsoid with a dark top as an open basket or bowl.

A separate, modest metal-roofed cooking shelter stands behind the main fale.
Its ground-level umu contains irregular stones and a low leaf-covered mound;
it is not a generic deep underground pit. Keep the work area and approach
clear. A preparation surface, hollow bowls, leaf packets and a small fuel
store suggest a coherent interrupted task. This is an interpretation of
domestic work, not cooking or fire-safety instruction.

The small enclosed living room has an actual doorway and recessed, framed
windows. Give any visible opening a furnished interior beyond it, with
plausible privacy and a clear threshold. Do not present a closed decorative
box as another connected space. A modest photograph or calendar, if visible,
needs approved image content, physical paper and attribution.

A cared-for yard, worn routes, fitted stone edges, coconut palms, banana
plants and modest household planting connect the buildings. Resolve stems,
leaf bases, root contact, uneven leaf ages and the transition between planting
and swept ground. A distant coast is not required: the household is the
subject, not a beach panorama. Do not add water simply to make the scene exotic.

Oblique daylight should describe the post rhythm and the contrast between
roof shade and bright grounds. Keep the open interior readable without
flattening its shadows. Leaf and screen movement remains anchored and
asynchronous, with a genuine reduced-motion freeze. Any cooking steam must
be sparse, source-motivated and free of transparent intersections. Use the
shared opt-in recorded ambience; distinguish illustrative recordings from
Samoan field recordings and never imply unidentified birds are local species.

## View sequence

1. `room`: the open household room, mats, screens and connected grounds.
2. `joinery`: the overhead timber structure and coconut-fibre bindings.
3. `weaving`: open utility baskets, prepared strips and coconut shells.
4. `cooking`: the separate shelter, ground-level umu and preparation area.
5. `home`: the real doorway into a quieter furnished room.
6. `grounds`: the return toward the main fale through maintained planting.

## Evidence and rejection criteria

Inspect arrival/composition, every connected space and the closest supported
construction and object views. Include the reverse of the buildings, the
underside of roof edges, stair corners, woven cavities, screen suspension,
plant contact and both sides of any opening. A centerline ray is not a
route-width or head-clearance test.

Reject a generic gazebo silhouette, polygonal dome, paper-thin roof, metal
ribbing that changes scale arbitrarily, decorative ropes passing through
timber, uniform wood cylinders, grass through floors, floating mats, sealed
vessels and simplified leaf blobs. Reject resort furniture and ceremonial
objects used as unexamined decoration. Correct structure and relationships
before adding clutter.

Preserve the first inspectable build, its source and original cameras.
Follow the initial inspection with at least three complete correction and
reinspection rounds for both representations. Compare performance with the
same cameras and viewport; batch or instance compatible geometry rather
than removing the detail required by close views.

## Sources and uncertainty

- [Te Papa, House (faletele) building, Samoa, C.001446](https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/112633),
  Thomas Andrew, 1890–1910: a partly thatched frame reveals construction
  depth and the work of a tufuga fai fale. The museum identifies a large
  meeting house, not an ordinary contemporary dwelling. The source photograph
  has a No Known Copyright Restrictions statement.
- [E. S. Craighill Handy and Willowdean Chatterson Handy, _Samoan house
  building, cooking, and tattooing_, Bishop Museum Bulletin 15, 1924](https://archive.org/details/samoanhousebuild00hand):
  pages 4–15 and plates I–IV distinguish long and round house framing,
  braces, screens, ordinary mats and actual Samoan lashings. Page 19 onward
  and plate V document cooking. These are historical observations, not a
  present-day social prescription. The scan's university contributor records
  it as out of copyright. Reproduction quality limits fine joint measurement.
- [UNESCO, ‘Ie Samoa, fine mat and its cultural value](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ie-samoa-fine-mat-and-its-cultural-value-01499):
  fine pandanus mats have important ceremonial and relational value. Do not
  reproduce their feathered hems as ordinary floor covering. The UNESCO
  photographs are not approved runtime assets.
- [Fale west of Apia, Upolu](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fale_Upolu.JPG),
  Roundtheworld, CC BY-SA 3.0: contemporary roof panels, eaves, platform,
  planting and open interior.
- [Lelepa fale, Savai'i, 2009](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Samoan_fale,_Lelepa_village,_Savaii,_Samoa_2009.JPG),
  Teinesavaii, CC BY-SA 3.0: folded screens, steps and stone facing under a
  metal roof. This is a different island and a round meeting-house reference,
  not this compound's identity or plan.
- [Woven coconut baskets](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Woven_coconut_baskets_-_panoramio.jpg),
  Peter Gill / UK, CC BY 3.0: broad crossing leaf strips, folded mouths,

- [Household portrait, Auckland Museum](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_of_women_holding_coconuts_in_front_of_dwelling_(AM_88236-1).jpg>),
  Tudor Washington Collins, CC BY 4.0: layered pola, coarse baskets, husks,
  floor pebbles and a stone threshold. The people and dwelling are not copied
  or claimed as the fictional household.
- [Umu in Samoa, removing rocks](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Umu_in_Samoa_-_removing_rocks.jpeg),
  Polynesian Xplorer, CC BY 2.0: irregular heated stones, prepared leaf packets
