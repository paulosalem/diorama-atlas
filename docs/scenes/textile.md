# Oaxaca Textile Workshop

**Scene:** `textile` · **Region:** Oaxaca, Mexico

[Visit the workshop](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=textile&inspect=1)

## Cultural intention

Make textile work legible as a chain of ecological knowledge, preparation,
tension, rhythm and accumulated skill. The workshop is an original
interpretation, not a claim to represent every Oaxacan weaving community.

## Spatial and sensory direction

A substantial wooden loom, hanging dyed skeins, cloth and a pigment/work table
organize the main room. A real passage continues into the dye/work area.
Warm daylight should reveal fibre and timber while saturated textiles remain
purposeful accents rather than filling every surface with pattern.

Loom rails, warp, working components, cloth edges and loose fibres must survive
close inspection. Shears, bowls, partly handled material and sample imagery
suggest an interrupted task. Preserve gentle textile movement and a quiet
illustrative work soundscape, with no suggestion of a documented performance.

## View sequence

`room` → `loom` → `dyes` → `yarn` → `passage`.

## Preserve and extend

The visitor should understand why objects are near one another. Add a new tool
only when its role and scale are credible. Do not invent meanings for motifs or
turn the workshop into a generic souvenir display.

The refinement adds shaped timber and joint pegs, ratchets and pawls, pulley
supports, eyed heddles and a recessed boat shuttle containing a wound bobbin.
Hanging skeins loop over their support and vary in length and fullness. Wet yarn
at a dye bowl and winding spools connect preparation to weaving without filling
the walking route. The inspected open-licensed photograph
[Telar en Oaxaca](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Telar_en_Oaxaca.jpg)
(Huitzillopochtli, CC BY-SA 4.0) informed thread tension and heddle construction;
it is a reference, not a copied workshop layout or a bundled runtime image.
