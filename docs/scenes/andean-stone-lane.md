# Andean Stone Lane

**Scene:** `andean-stone-lane` · **Region:** Andean highlands, Peru

[Visit the lane](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=andean-stone-lane&inspect=1)

## Cultural intention

Explore a layered street: fitted stone construction and later plaster/timber
architecture remain visibly different while sharing a lived urban space.
The lane is invented, with no claim to reproduce a celebrated wall or stone.

## Spatial and sensory direction

Individually shaped masonry, inclined surfaces, close paving, upper façades and
balconies make bodily scale and changing thresholds important. A continued
passage should imply a neighborhood beyond the first frame.

Geometric shape must carry fitted joints; a texture alone cannot substitute for
them. Use oblique light to reveal edges and shallow relief. Vary stone grain
and restrained wear while avoiding regularly repeated block shapes or artificial
gaps that undermine the construction.

## View sequence

`arrival` → `stone` → `passage` → `balconies`.

## Preserve and extend

Keep the contrast between architectural layers rather than blending everything
into a generic "ancient" street. Do not copy famous masonry arrangements or
assign the invented lane a specific archaeological provenance.

Stone faces now resolve narrow shoulders, shallow tool-like depressions and
fine relief inside the original fitted footprints. Preserve those shapes rather
than restoring uniform broad bevels. Spatial batches share one vertex-tinted
material; distance-selected geometry restores the full surface before approach.
The original lane, stair passage and named cameras remain the spatial reference.
A few doorway leaves move subtly about their attached bases; the pot and stems
remain still, and reduced motion freezes the movement.

The miniature retains real lower-storey back walls, returns and floors beneath
the upper houses. Gable profiles meet the roof pitch and the roofs sit over
their bearing walls. Preserve these complete volumes on reverse and underside
views without closing the lane or the stepped side passage. Detailed masonry
must be physically sectioned with its building, not left projecting beyond
the ground after a distance-detail group is cropped.
