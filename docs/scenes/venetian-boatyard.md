# Venetian Boatyard Canal

**Scene:** `venetian-boatyard` · **Region:** Venetian Lagoon, Italy

[Visit the boatyard](https://paulosalem.github.io/diorama-atlas/?scene=venetian-boatyard&inspect=1)

## Cultural intention

Explore a squero as the working meeting of land, timber craft and lagoon water.
Boatbuilding and repair—not a postcard landmark—give this anonymous yard
its reason to exist.

## Spatial and sensory direction

Curved wooden hulls, supports, ropes, a slipway, sheds and neighboring masonry
establish a usable work sequence. Hull interiors and fittings must be actual
geometry; boats and benches must rest on credible supports.

Differentiate dry handled timber, repeatedly wet stone and selectively
weathered edges. Canal reflections should respond to the local flow without
regular stripes or an implausibly rough sea surface. The workshop and water
remain connected spatially rather than divided into unrelated display zones.

## View sequence

`arrival` → `gondola` → `workshop` → `canal`.

## Preserve and extend

Use boat references for construction and proportions, not unsupported claims of
a surveyed historical vessel. Add repair materials only where a task explains
them. Do not label the illustrative audio palette as a recording of the yard.

The side workshop now has a real doorway and modeled interior with benches,
timber, bent ribs, shelves and a practical lamp. Door leaves fold away from the
opening; preserve person-scale clearance through it. Oars use changing shaft
and blade cross-sections, clamps have jaws and screw detail, and curled ribbon
shavings accompany a finishing tin and brush. Keep the existing hulls, slipway,
water and all four original cameras.

Background-house roofs belong to their actual bearing footprints. Miniature
cropping must not leave roof strips suspended beyond an omitted house; retain
the supported roof and complete building sections without narrowing the canal
or sacrificing the boatyard's useful close views.
