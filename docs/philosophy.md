# A philosophy of small places

## Cultural enrichment through attention

A room is more than its furniture. A courtyard can express privacy and welcome;
a field can reveal the cooperation behind water; a shop can arrange knowledge
into drawers, scales and folded paper. Such relationships make culture tangible.

The Atlas gives visitors time to notice them. Its ambition is to make unfamiliar
places approachable without flattening their differences: to awaken curiosity,
broaden the vocabulary of everyday life, and encourage further encounters with
the people and traditions represented. Cultural enrichment is not collecting
destinations. It is discovering more ways to understand how people inhabit a
world together.

The experience should reward both a brief visit and patient observation.
Beauty welcomes the visitor; detail sustains attention; explanation gives the
detail meaning. None can replace the others.

## Interpret places, do not impersonate them

The scenes are anonymous, authored interpretations of regional traditions.
They are not surveyed buildings, photogrammetric records, reconstructed
businesses or definitive historical accounts. The map marks a region of
inspiration, not the address of an invented place.

Reference photographs document actual places and retain accurate attribution.
The scene may combine compatible observations into an original composition,
adding charm and atmosphere without inventing cultural authority. A tasteful
artistic decision still needs plausible construction, use and context.

Avoid a universal visual shorthand for "traditional" or "exotic." Materials,
climate, working practices and local histories must distinguish the settings.
Neither a famous monument nor a handful of decorative motifs stands for a whole
culture. Do not invent sacred inscriptions, medical advice, ritual meanings or
claims of community endorsement.

Texts should distinguish observed context from authored interpretation. Prefer
institutional, scholarly and firsthand sources, including local voices, where
available. Acknowledge uncertainty and conflicting accounts. The Atlas is a
starting point for learning, not a replacement for those sources or for visiting
and listening respectfully.

## Presence before spectacle

The visitor should feel inside a place that continues beyond the frame.
An open door must lead somewhere; a drawer must contain a cavity; a tool should
relate to a task. Occlusion, parallax, bodily scale and coherent light make
space believable in ways that an impressive object count cannot.

Small imperfections are part of this coherence. Timber varies even when cared
for. Stone carries grain before it carries damage. Wear accumulates where
hands, feet, weather and maintenance explain it. A little disorder can suggest
someone has just stepped away; indiscriminate dirt suggests neglect instead.

The aspiration is photographic believability **and** a convincing feeling of
place. The present collection remains recognizably real-time 3D. Technical
success does not establish photorealism, cultural accuracy or human approval.

## A calm, hospitable interface

The interface should recede so the place can lead. Offer useful viewpoints,
unobtrusive explanations and readable controls, including compact and landscape
layouts. Narration complements looking; it must not cover the subject or compete
with another caption.

English and Brazilian Portuguese are equal entry points to the entire
experience, not merely alternate audio tracks. Respect reduced motion, saved
mute choices and browser playback restrictions. Music and ambience default to
enabled preferences, but a first visit waits for explicit playback. Narration
remains a separate choice. Hidden pages stop sounding.

## An enduring, inspectable collection

The public app should work without an account, a paid provider or a developer's
cache. Bundle approved assets and preserve their rights, sources and processing
history. Disclose AI-generated imagery and voices rather than presenting them
as documentary evidence.

Future contributors and agents should inherit a clear direction, not a
collection of opaque experiments. Each scene has a brief; shared mechanisms
have one owner; changes have reproducible evidence. Keep maintenance tools
reusable and separate scene-specific choices from their implementation.

the [scene briefs](scenes/README.md) for local intent, and
